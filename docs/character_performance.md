# CHARACTER PERFORMANCE SHEET: PROF. DOT
## 2D Character Animation Blueprint & Lip-Sync Direction

**Character Identity:** Prof. Dot  
**Role:** On-screen animated presenter, academic companion, and visual guide  
**Archetype:** Curious, friendly, enthusiastic, mathematically rigorous, approachable academic mentor  
**Visual Style:** Minimalist Swiss-vector aesthetic with hand-drawn sketch flourishes (clean contours, dot-matrix eyes, expressive brows, stylus accessory, stylish round academic glasses)  
**Voice Source:** Student's actual vocal recording (no synthetic AI voices; 100% synchronized to physical student recording)

---

## 1. CORE PERFORMANCE PRINCIPLES

1. **Balanced Screen Presence (Math First):**
   - Prof. Dot does *not* hog the camera. When complex coordinate grid transformations or dense algebraic steps take center stage, Dot gracefully steps aside, lowers posture, or transitions off-screen.
   - Screen presence ratio: approximately 40% on-screen presenting, 30% edge-of-frame gesturing, and 30% off-screen (pure mathematics and geometric animation priority).

2. **Gaze & Eye Contact Hierarchy:**
   - **Direct Address (60%):** Looking directly into the camera lens when posing philosophical or intuitive questions ("What if it's an action?", "Sounds impossible, doesn't it?").
   - **Referential Focus (35%):** Looking directly at the mathematical entity being manipulated (watching vector $\mathbf{e}_1$ ascend, inspecting matrix columns).
   - **Deliberate Beat Pauses (5%):** Looking down thoughtfully or raising eyes in an "aha!" realization before delivering a key punchline.

3. **Resting State & Micro-Movements:**
   - Even when listening or during pauses, Dot is never a static frozen sprite.
   - Includes subtle idle breathing (1.5% scale breathing oscillation every 3 seconds), gentle weight shifts between feet, and natural blinking every 3.5 to 5.0 seconds.

## 4. GESTURAL VOCABULARY & KINEMATICS

- **G01: Stylus Tap & Spark:** Dot holds a sleek dark stylus in his dominant hand. Tapping an on-screen math object causes an interactive wave or highlight. (Used in Scene 01 to break the static matrix, and Scene 04 to confirm linearity).
- **G02: The Horizon Reveal (Open Palm Sweep):** Both arms extend outward from chest level, palms facing up, sweeping smoothly outwards. (Used in Scene 01 and Scene 06 to indicate "space itself").
- **G03: The Finger Snap Pivot:** Right hand rises to shoulder height, performs a crisp finger snap, followed immediately by an index-finger point at the standard basis vectors. (Used in Scene 02).
- **G04: The Anchor Pin (Firm Palm Down):** Left hand presses firmly downward in a grounding motion while pointing at vector $\mathbf{e}_2$ to emphasize its immobility during shear. (Used in Scene 03).
- **G05: The Blueprint Unfurl:** Two hands hold imaginary cards, pushing them outward left and right to present Additivity and Homogeneity. (Used in Scene 04).
- **G06: The Conductor’s Guide:** Arm moves in a smooth arc from the calculation box across to the matrix bracket, visually escorting vector $(1, 1)$ into Column 1. (Used in Scene 05).
- **G07: The Architect’s Bow & Wave:** Hand pushes glasses gently up the bridge of the nose, followed by a friendly wave and bow. (Used in Scene 06).
